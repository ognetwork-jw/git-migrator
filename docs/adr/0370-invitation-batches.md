# ADR-0370: Invitation batches: lifecycle, the AUTH-061 guarantee, endpoints and staleness

- Status: accepted (spec updated)
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Task: T-085
- Affects: AUTH-060, AUTH-061, AUTH-050, API-020, UI-029, JOB-060, LIF-020, LIF-021

## Context

AUTH-060 describes the flow in prose: a draft from candidates, a seat preview, deselection with a reason, approval that enqueues a send job, and acceptance correlation at inventory. It leaves open how the safety property of AUTH-061 ("nothing is ever invited outside an approved batch") is enforced, how a retry avoids a second invitation, what the web process (which has no provider access) can do, which states a person must be in to be invited, and how mapping changes interact with staleness. An invitation is visible to a person and cannot be taken back in bulk, so these points are decided conservatively.

## Decision

1. **Candidates.** A candidate is a human source Identity with a non-empty e-mail whose mapping row exists and is `unmapped`, who has no outstanding entry (`selected` or `sent`, in any batch of any Route with the same target Endpoint), and whose normalised address (`lower(trim(email))`) no outstanding entry in the same target organization holds (Round 4 review). This is the `invitationCandidates` of the route index (ADR-0311) minus what batches hold. Two candidates sharing an address are never drafted together (422 for named people; for `all`, the first by id). `suggested` people are not candidates: an operator confirms or rejects the suggestion first. A draft names its people (`identityIds`) or asks for `all`, never both, at most 5,000 entries. Each entry stores the e-mail and the target team slugs of the person's source groups (the target team's slug when the Group Mapping is confirmed, the planned slug otherwise).
2. **Seat preview.** The web process cannot call the provider, so creating a draft enqueues the job step `seats`, which reads `org.seatInfo()` and stores `{toInvite, seatsTotal, seatsFilled, seatsReadAt}` in `seatPreview`. Until then, or when the provider tells nothing, the UI shows "seats unknown". `toInvite` is kept equal to the number of `selected` entries by every select, deselect and approval. A seat total of 0 is how the provider says "no limit".
3. **Deselecting** requires a reason (1 to 500 characters), is allowed only in a `draft` batch, and creates the eight `identity_excluded` Expected Differences of AUTH-050 step 4 for the person's source Provider id. They are linked to the mapping (`identity_mapping_id`, ADR-0320) and, new here, to the entry (`expected_difference.invitation_id`). Reselecting revokes exactly the Expected Differences linked to the entry, so an exclusion the operator made on the mapping itself is never revoked by a reselect. Confirming, excluding or unmapping the mapping revokes everything linked to it, entries included, as in ADR-0320. No Expected Difference is created for a person who is already decided (`confirmed`, `excluded`, `pending_invite`): masking them would hide real differences. Deselecting changes nothing that mapping resolution returns, so it marks no Analysis stale.
4. **Approval** locks the batch row (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE`) and the Route's mapping lock, requires `draft`, and re-reads the mappings of the selected entries. Entries whose person is no longer invitable are moved to `deselected` with the reason "no longer a candidate (<status>)" and no exclusion. The body must carry `expectedToken` (a hash of the selected entry ids, shown with the batch; what the operator confirmed in the dialog, frozen when it opened; a missing token is a 422) and may carry `expectedCount`; a changed selection or a different final count is a 409 and nothing changes. Zero entries is a 409. The batch becomes `approved` with the approver and time in one conditional update (`WHERE status = 'draft'`), so two approvals at once end with one 202 and one 409. The send step is enqueued after the commit; if the queue is down the approval stands and the next inventory pass of the target Endpoint schedules the send step of every `approved` or `sending` batch again.
5. **AUTH-061 is enforced three times.** (a) The API only creates `approved` batches through the approval above. (b) The send job re-reads the batch under a row lock at every item and stops unless the status is `approved` or `sending` with an approver and approval time; it sends only items that are `selected` in that batch, and re-reads the item's mapping: a person who is now `confirmed`, `excluded` or `pending_invite` is not invited and the item fails with `mapping_<status>` (an item whose earlier attempt may have reached the provider never fails: see point 7). (c) Database triggers: an `invitation` becomes `sent` only from `selected` in a batch approved by an Actor and `approved`/`sending`; nothing becomes `selected` or `deselected` outside a draft; the e-mail, teams, person and batch of an approved batch cannot change; a batch is inserted as a draft without an approval, becomes `approved` only with its approver, cannot go back to `draft`, from `sending` to `approved`, or out of `sent`/`partial` (except `sent` to `partial`), and its approval cannot change.
6. **At most one outstanding invitation per person and per address in a target organization, enforced by the system.** The database holds two partial unique indexes on `invitation` over `(target_endpoint_id, source_identity_id)` and `(target_endpoint_id, email_normalised)` where `status NOT IN ('deselected', 'failed', 'expired', 'accepted')`. An entry is outstanding while it can still be sent (`selected`, which only exists in a batch that is not finished), the provider holds its invitation (`sent`), or the system cannot rule out that it does (`unknown`), so no denormalised flag is needed. The predicate names the ended states because PostgreSQL cannot use an enum value in the transaction that adds it (`unknown` is added in the same migration). `route_id`, `target_endpoint_id` and `email_normalised` are set by the guard trigger from the batch, its Route and the e-mail, whoever writes; `target_endpoint_id` is fixed when the entry is created. The API checks the same things first so the operator gets a 409/422 and not a constraint error: drafting excludes held people and addresses, and reselecting an entry whose person or address another outstanding entry holds is a 409. Nothing relies on the provider refusing a duplicate.
7. **Idempotent sending.** The claim runs under the Route mapping lock and, in the transaction that sets `send_started_at`, moves the mapping with a conditional `UPDATE ... WHERE status = 'unmapped'` to `pending_invite` (and marks the Route's Analyses stale, ADR-0310, FAC-006). Only an `unmapped` person is invited; anyone else fails the entry with `mapping_<status>` (`mapping_missing` without a row). On a claim without an earlier attempt, a definitive refusal or a rate limit from `invite()` gives the claim back (the mapping returns to `unmapped`; the entry fails, or waits with `send_started_at` cleared); any other error keeps the claim, and the retry looks the invitation up. The entry leaves `selected` only together with the provider's invitation id, in one conditional update. An entry that carries `send_started_at` on a later run may have reached the provider without being recorded; that run never posts and never fails the entry (see Round 3 review). It looks for the entry's own invitation (`findOwnInvitation`: the same normalised address, created at or after the first `send_started_at`, recorded on no other entry; pending before failed) and records what it finds. A member of the target with the invited address (the only one, not confirmed for another person) is the invitee: the entry is recorded and linked (`accepted`). **If it finds nothing it does not post again, and it does not free anyone**: the entry becomes `unknown` (error `unknown_outcome`), the mapping stays `pending_invite`, and the person and the address stay held (the indexes count `unknown`) until an operator calls `POST /invitation-batches/{id}/items/{itemId}/resolve` with `{outcome: 'invited' | 'not_invited'}` (`manageInvitations`, audited): `invited` makes the entry `sent` (provider id unknown; the inventory links the invitee later), `not_invited` makes it `failed`, returns the mapping to `unmapped` and marks the Route's Analyses stale. The lookup is outside the `invite()` error handling: a fault in it (a limit, `forbidden`, a timeout) leaves the claim untouched (the entry stays `selected` with `send_started_at`, the mapping stays `pending_invite`); a limit sets `next_attempt_at` and reschedules, any other fault fails the job for BullMQ to retry. Only a rate limit from `invite()` itself on a claim that is not a retry clears `send_started_at`. One run per batch holds a session advisory lock. A definitive refusal (`invalid`, `forbidden`, `conflict`, `unsupported`, `not_found`, `blocked_by_provider`) fails only that entry, storing the adapter's code and HTTP status, never provider text.
8. **Rate limits.** A `rate_limited` error leaves the remaining items `selected`, stores `next_attempt_at` (the error's `retryAt`, else 24 h) and enqueues the send step with that delay under a separate dedupe id (the running job still holds the immediate one). A job that wakes early does nothing (`not-due`). The batch ends `sent`, or `partial` when an item `failed` or `expired`.
9. **Acceptance and expiry** are decided at the inventory of the target Endpoint (`correlateInvitations`, after the Identities are up to date), see ADR-0371.
10. **Revoke.** `POST /invitation-batches/{id}/items/{itemId}/revoke` (the revoke flow that `unmap` of a `pending_invite` mapping refers to, ADR-0320) queues the job step `revoke`, which cancels the pending invitation through the adapter (`InvitationWriter.cancel`), sets the item to `expired` with the reason `revoked`, returns the mapping to `unmapped` and marks the Route stale. When the provider no longer had the invitation, the job first checks the target members: a member with the stored login or the invited address means it was accepted, and the entry is linked (`accepted`) instead of released; otherwise it is `expired` with `revoked_not_found`. An expiry also turns a `sent` batch `partial`. The audit row is written after the step is queued.
11. **Endpoints** (all under `/api/v1`; reads need `read`, commands `manageInvitations`): `GET /routes/{id}/invitation-candidates`, `POST /routes/{id}/invitation-batches`, `GET /invitation-batches` (`routeId`, `status`, cursor), `GET /invitation-batches/{id}` (the batch with counts and a page of entries, optionally filtered by status), `POST /invitation-batches/{id}/items/{itemId}/{select|deselect|revoke}`, `POST /invitation-batches/{id}/approve`. The spec lists only the three commands; the reads are what UI-029 needs. A sent entry lists up to five new target members (created after the invitation was sent, not confirmed for anyone) as suggestions, an e-mail match first (AUTH-060 step 5.2); confirming one through the existing mapping `confirm` endpoint on a `pending_invite` mapping also marks the invitation `accepted`.
12. **Events and audit.** Every change publishes `invitation.updated` with `ids.invitation = <batchId>` (topics `invitation:<id>` and `list:invitations`, ADR-0270), in the transaction for API writes and after commit for jobs; a change that marks Analyses stale also publishes `migration.updated` without ids. Create, approve, select, deselect and revoke write `AuditEvent`s without e-mail addresses.

## Alternatives

- A new `sending` item status or a separate outbox table for claims: rejected, `send_started_at` plus the e-mail lookup gives the same guarantee without changing the InvitationStatus enum of the data model.
- Holding a database transaction (and the batch row lock) across the provider calls: rejected, a slow provider would block approvals and edits of the batch and exhaust connections.
- Reading seats in the API process: rejected, the web process has no provider credentials (ARC-020).
- Letting the matching cascade (AUTH-050 step 2) also decide `pending_invite` mappings: rejected, AUTH-050 says decisions are never overwritten by it.
- Marking Analyses stale on every deselect: rejected, resolution is unchanged, and the cost grows with the Route.

## Lock order and the mapping endpoints (round 1 review)

- **One lock order everywhere:** the invitation lock of the target Endpoint (`invitation-target:<endpointId>`, several in id order where an entry's fixed target differs from its Route's current one; Round 4 review), then the Route mapping lock (`identity-mapping:<routeId>`, the key of ADR-0320), then the batch row (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE`), then the invitation row, then the mapping row, then Migration rows in id order (inside `markAnalysesStale`, ADR-0310). Draft, select, deselect, approve, the send claim, recording, giving a claim back, revoke and the inventory correlation all follow it. The provider is never called while a transaction is open.
- **Mapping endpoints:** `exclude` of a `pending_invite` mapping, and CSV `map` or `exclude` rows for one, are refused (409 `revoke_first`, CSV row error `invite_pending`), as `unmap` already is. `confirm` stays allowed: it is how an operator links the invitee.
- **Reselecting** an entry also needs the person to be `unmapped` still; approval drops entries whose person is not (reason `no longer a candidate (<status>)`, no exclusion).

## Round 2 review

- **Address normalisation** has one definition: spaces trimmed at both ends and ASCII letters lower-cased. The guard trigger does it in SQL (`translate(btrim(email), 'A-Z', 'a-z')`) and `normaliseEmail` in `@git-migrator/jobs` does the same in JavaScript; the API imports that function.
- A write that reaches one of the two outstanding-invitation indexes (`23505`) answers 409 `conflict`, not a server error.
- The batch guard also freezes `route_id`.
- Lock order, completed: correlation and expiry take the batch row after the Route lock; `confirm` of a `pending_invite` mapping locks the person's batch rows and invitation rows before the mapping row. Confirming also accepts `unknown` entries.

## Round 3 review

- **The retry lookup only takes the entry's own invitation.** `send_started_at` is stamped once, by the first claim, and kept on every retry. A listed invitation counts for a retried entry only when its address is the same normalised address, the provider created it at or after `send_started_at` (compared to the whole second, the provider's precision), and no other entry of the same target Endpoint records its id. Pending wins over failed. An older failed invitation for the address (an earlier batch's, or one made outside the system) is therefore never recorded as this entry's outcome, so the inventory cannot expire the entry from someone else's report and free the person. Clock skew between the worker and the provider can only make the lookup miss, which leaves the entry `unknown` (held). The adapter contract gains optional `createdAt` on `listPending` and `createdAt`/`failedAt` on `listFailed` (ADR-0372); without `createdAt` nothing matches.
- **A retried claim never fails.** Whatever happened to the mapping meanwhile (confirmed, excluded, missing), the claim of an entry with `send_started_at` runs the same lookup (own pending or failed invitation, then a single member with the address) and records what it finds. If it finds nothing the entry becomes `unknown` with `error = 'mapping_<status>'` (`unknown_outcome` when the mapping is still `pending_invite`). An `unmapped` mapping under a retried claim is moved back to `pending_invite`. Only a claim without `send_started_at` can fail with `mapping_<status>`.
- **Confirming during a send.** `confirm` of a `pending_invite` mapping also moves the person's `selected` entries that carry `send_started_at` to `unknown` (`error = 'mapping_confirmed'`): the call may be in flight or its response lost, so the address stays held until an operator resolves the entry. The send job's later `recordSent` and `releaseClaim` only touch `selected` entries and `pending_invite`/`invite` mappings, so they change nothing after that.
- **Entries without a provider id.** An entry resolved as `invited` is `sent` without a provider id. The inventory correlation first runs `findOwnInvitation` for it (with `send_started_at`, else `sent_at`): a pending hit stores the id and the invitee login, a failed hit stores the id and expires the entry, no hit goes through the "gone from the pending list" path (acceptance by login or by the unique e-mail, otherwise `unresolved` after seven days). Revoke looks such an entry's pending invitation up the same way and cancels it; when nothing is found it takes the member check of `cancelled: false` (accepted when the invitee is a member, otherwise `expired` with `revoked_not_found`, an operator's decision). The batch view marks such entries (`providerIdKnown: false` on the item), and its revoke dialog warns that on a miss the person and the address are released.
- **A retry never links by login.** `linkUnrecorded` matches the invited address only: a `selected` entry has no `invitee_login`, which is written only when an invitation is recorded (`recordSent`) or by the correlation of a `sent` entry. A login the provider reports with a pending invitation is stored when the lookup records that invitation. `linkUnrecorded` checks first that the member is not confirmed for another person; when it is, nothing is written and the entry becomes `unknown` (never `sent` without an id).

### Sweep: every way out of "outstanding"

Outstanding means `selected`, `sent` or `unknown` (the two unique indexes count them, per target organization). For every transition to `failed`, `expired`, `deselected` or `accepted`, and every move of a mapping to `unmapped`, either no invitation of the system can be outstanding for the person and the address, or an operator decided it explicitly.

| Transition | Where | Why it is safe |
|---|---|---|
| `selected` to `deselected` (deselect) | API, draft batch only | Nothing is sent from a draft: the claim requires `approved`/`sending` and the guard trigger refuses `sent` in a draft. |
| `selected` to `deselected` (dropped at approval) | API, draft batch, in the approving transaction | Same: the batch is still a draft. |
| `selected` to `failed` `mapping_<status>` | send claim | Only for an entry without `send_started_at`: no attempt of it reached `invite()` (the stamp is written in the claim transaction before the call and cleared only after a rate limit, which created nothing). |
| `selected` to `failed` (definitive refusal) | send, after `invite()` | Only on a claim without `send_started_at` before it. Writes are never retried by the HTTP client (`retry: false`), so this was the one POST, and the provider refused it: it created nothing. A 422 because an invitation for the address already exists is not a refusal: the adapter returns that pending invitation and it is recorded. |
| `unknown` to `failed` `resolved_not_invited` | API `resolve` | Operator decision (`manageInvitations`, audited). |
| `sent` to `expired` `failed:*` | inventory correlation | The provider itself reports this very invitation as failed or expired: its recorded id, which comes only from `invite()`, from the adapter's 422 lookup, or from `findOwnInvitation` (created after this entry's first attempt, recorded nowhere else). A failed invitation cannot be accepted any more. |
| `sent` to `expired` `revoked` | revoke job | Operator decision; the provider confirmed that it removed the invitation. |
| `sent` to `expired` `revoked_not_found` | revoke job | Operator decision; the provider has no pending invitation with the id (or, without an id, none of its own for the address), and no member is the invitee. |
| `sent` to `accepted` | inventory correlation, revoke job | The invitation is no longer pending, not failed, and the invitee is a member (stored login, or the single member with the address under `autoConfirmEmail` in correlation). An accepted invitation is not outstanding. |
| `selected` to `accepted` | send retry (`linkUnrecorded`) | No own invitation is pending or failed, and the single member with the invited address (not confirmed for anyone else) is in the organization: the invitation, if one was created, was accepted. The mapping becomes `confirmed`, so the person is not a candidate again. |
| `sent`/`unknown` to `accepted` | mapping `confirm` | Operator decision: the operator names the invitee. In-flight `selected` entries become `unknown` instead (above). |
| mapping `pending_invite` to `unmapped` | `releaseClaim` | Only with the definitive refusal or the rate limit from `invite()` on a claim without an earlier attempt: nothing was created. After a rate limit the entry stays `selected` and holds the person and the address. |
| mapping `pending_invite` to `unmapped` | `expireItem` | Together with the three `expired` transitions above. |
| mapping `pending_invite` to `unmapped` | API `resolve` `not_invited` | Operator decision (only when the mapping is still `pending_invite`). |
| mapping to `unmapped` elsewhere | mapping service, CSV import, inventory | Never from `pending_invite`: `unmap`, `exclude` and CSV `map`/`exclude` are refused for it (409 / `invite_pending`), CSV `invite` keeps it, and the matching cascade only rewrites `unmapped` and `suggested` mappings. `confirm` moves it to `confirmed`, never to `unmapped`. |

Nothing else changes an entry's status: the batch guard trigger refuses every other move once a batch is approved, and no code deletes entries or batches.

## Round 4 review: the guarantee is per target organization

An invitation lives in the target organization, and several Routes may target the same Endpoint. Holds kept per Route let two Routes invite the same person or address into the same organization, so the provider's duplicate refusal was the only thing preventing a second invitation. The guarantee is now **at most one outstanding invitation per person and per address in a target organization**.

- **Data.** `invitation.target_endpoint_id` (a foreign key to `endpoint`) is set by the guard trigger from the batch's Route when the entry is created and kept on every update (a writer cannot change it). Both outstanding partial unique indexes are keyed on `(target_endpoint_id, source_identity_id)` and `(target_endpoint_id, email_normalised)`. The migration is unreleased, so the change is in `20261008000020`.
- **API.** `candidates`, `holder()`, drafting and reselecting check outstanding entries in the Route's target organization on every Route. A named person who is held is refused with the Route and batch that hold them (`held by an outstanding invitation (Route <id>, batch <id>)`), and so is a reselect (409).
- **Locks.** Every invitation write first takes the invitation lock of the target Endpoint, then the Route mapping lock. The API's draft, select, deselect, approve and resolve take it, and so do the send claim, record, release, unknown, correlation and revoke in the jobs. Mapping `confirm` takes the locks of the Route's target and of the person's entries' targets before the Route lock. Two drafts on two Routes into one organization are therefore serialized, and the second one sees the first one's entries.
- **A Route that moves to another target.** Config sync may change a Route's target. An entry keeps the organization it was drafted for: approval drops entries whose target is no longer the Route's (`no longer a candidate (target changed)`). The send claim fails an unsent one (`target_changed`) and holds a retried one as `unknown` (`target_changed`) without any provider call. Correlation selects `sent` entries by their own `target_endpoint_id`, and revoke connects to the entry's organization. `findOwnInvitation` matches "recorded elsewhere" on `target_endpoint_id`.

### Sweep of invitation queries filtered by Route

| Query | Scope | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Candidates: held people and addresses | target | AUTH-061 per organization (changed). |
| Candidates: mapping status | Route | The Identity Mapping is per Route (AUTH-050). |
| Drafting: duplicate addresses within the batch | batch | One batch is on one Route and one target. |
| Draft and reselect refusal (`holder()`) | target | Changed. |
| Approval: mapping re-read and drops | Route, plus the target check | The mapping is per Route; entries drafted for another target are dropped. |
| Send claim and `releaseClaim`: the mapping | Route | The claim moves the mapping of the batch's Route. |
| Retry lookup and correlation: "recorded on another entry" | target | Provider ids are unique per organization (changed in round 3, now on the column). |
| Correlation: `sent` entries to look at | target (the entry's own) | Changed from "batches of the Routes that target the Endpoint now". |
| Correlation: rescheduling approved batches | Routes that target the Endpoint now | A batch is sent through its Route. |
| `resolve`, `expireItem`, `linkAccepted`: the mapping they move | Route | The mapping is per Route; the entry being settled is the only outstanding one in the organization. |
| Mapping `confirm`: entries it accepts or holds as `unknown` | Route | A `pending_invite` mapping on a Route comes only from a claim of that Route. Its outstanding entry is the only one for the person in the organization, so no entry of another Route can be involved. |
| Batch lists, batch detail, suggestions | Route | Views only. Suggestions skip targets confirmed on the same Route, as ADR-0320 does. |

The rows of the round-3 sweep table hold unchanged, now per target organization. Each move to a non-outstanding status touches the one outstanding entry the organization holds for the person and the address, and the indexes make sure there is no second one on another Route.

- **After an acceptance on one Route.** The person's mapping on another Route into the same organization is still `unmapped`. The same inventory pass that accepts the invitation lists the new member first, and the matching cascade then suggests or confirms that mapping by e-mail before correlation runs. If the member's address is private, a later draft on the other Route would POST for a person who is already a member. The provider refuses that POST, and no invitation is created.
